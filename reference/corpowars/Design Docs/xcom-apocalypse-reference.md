# X-COM: Apocalypse — Reference Notes for the City Layer (2026-09-26)

## Factions
- ~25 organisations in Mega-Primus. Government (the Senate, funds X-COM), Megapol (police), Cult of Sirius (hostile), plus corporations and service groups: Marsec, Transtellar, Superdynamics, General Metro, Cyberweb, Solmine, Sensovision, Lifetree, Nutrivend, Evonet, Sanctuary Clinic, Nanotech, Energen, Synthemesh, gangs (Psyke, Diablo, Osiron, Grav Ball League), S.E.L.F., Mutant Alliance, Extropians, Technocrats.
- Each org has a defence force with a size (about 7–16) and a tech level (1–4) that appears when its buildings are raided. The same stat pair would fit our subsidiary security profiles.
- Most orgs are defined by **what they sell or provide** to the player: vehicles, engines, Elerium, recruits (androids, hybrids), transport of staff. Hostility shuts off that supply. This gives each faction a mechanical reason to matter.

## Relations
- Tiers: Allied +75..+100, Friendly +25..+74, Neutral −24..+24, Unfriendly −49..−25, Hostile −100..−50.
- Starting values are randomised slightly per game (±10, plus more on higher difficulty).
- Third parties react to attacks: attacking a group's enemy raises your standing with that group.
- Bribes move an org one tier up. Heavily infiltrated orgs refuse bribes.
- Hostile consequences are specific per org. Hostile Megapol attacks your vehicles, a hostile Senate cuts funding immediately, and hostile Transtellar stops staff transfers.

## Buildings & economy
- Building types: corporate (Senate, HQ, offices), services, residential, industrial (factories, warehouse), utility (power, water, farms, spaceport), amenities, temples. Each has a single "home cell"; destroying it destroys the building.
- Damaging buildings angers their owner, puts people out of work and reduces government income. Damage cuts weekly profits.
- Organisation funds officially "don't affect gameplay". Players do raid orgs to weaken them.
- Stun raids let you loot a building without angering the owner. This is a known exploit, and a design lesson: tie our consequences to being identified or to damage done, not to the act itself.
- **Cut from the shipped game:** orgs buying and selling buildings based on their finances (dropped as too complex), org leaders who could be tailed, arrested or assassinated, a spy system (undercover infiltration of orgs, theft, assassination), a diplomacy screen for brokering alliances between orgs, and investigation skills (interrogation, forensics) used to uncover infiltration or extortion evidence.

## Alien infiltration (the hidden spreading influence)
- Aliens in a building influence its owner. Infiltration spreads to adjacent buildings over time. Its rate depends on building type (HQs and the Senate are the most vulnerable), how susceptible the org is (0% to 200%), and the alien species.
- At 100% infiltration the org embargoes the player, allies with the aliens, and its forces gain +3 tech level.

## Takeaways for Corpo Wars
1. Our property market and spy missions are close to Apocalypse's **cut** features. We'd be building the version they couldn't ship.
2. Give each corp a service tied to the player (Corvane blackouts, Halvorsen fence, Candor intel, Castellan weapons, Northstar recruits). Relations then matter mechanically, the way hostile orgs in Apocalypse stop selling to you.
3. Use five named relation tiers, randomise starting relations slightly per save, and have third parties react to attacks on their enemies.
4. A "hidden influence spreading between buildings" system could be a later layer. Shell subsidiaries and Lattice buyouts are candidates.
5. Org leaders as targets (tail, recruit, assassinate) fit our RECRUIT objective and could become executive contracts.
6. Guard force size and tech level per org is a proven, simple security model.
